01  The headline act
SEC.01  /  every breath leaves a trace in the EEG
<PKND_002.01.01>
PEER REVIEWEDEEGRESPIRATIONSLEEP

Quiet breathing evokes scalp EEG potentials in wakefulness and in every sleep stage

Grassion, Andrillon, … Similowski · Journal of Physiology · 28 Oct 2026

Using Cleveland Family Study polysomnography from 729 participants (median 7,871 respiratory cycles each), the authors averaged EEG time-locked to inspiration onset.

They found a pre-inspiratory component about 300 ms before inspiration and post-inspiratory peaks up to 3 s after. The potentials persisted in N2, N3 and REM sleep with a simplified, K-complex-like shape, alongside more spindle-range activity. They were present regardless of BMI and unchanged in the 203 participants without sleep-disordered breathing.

CAVEATSWhat these potentials mean physiologically or clinically is still unknown, and scalp EEG can't say where they come from. Polysomnography montages are sparse.
WHY IT
MATTERS
FOR YOU		A direct input to your brain–body coupling pipeline: respiration-locked averaging is a simple first analysis on your EEG + respiration recordings, and it gives a respiratory 'evoked' term to model or regress before reading oscillatory or aperiodic changes as purely cortical. Thomas Andrillon's post about it drew modest Bluesky attention.
REF / IDJ Physiol, 28 Oct 2026 · doi:10.1113/JP290737 · Andrillon on Bluesky

02  EEG
SEC.02  /  your core modality, plus your requested paper
<PKND_002.02.01>
REQUESTED BY YOUPEER REVIEWEDDCME/I

A canonical microcircuit DCM for estimating excitation/inhibition balance from ERPs

Hauke, Rodriguez-Sanchez, Oloye, Berndt, Pinotsis, Friston, Mathalon & Adams (UCL and others) · Translational Psychiatry · 31 Jul 2026 (open access)

Requested by you, so it's outside the usual one-week window. The authors extend the convolution-based canonical microcircuit (four populations: spiny stellate, superficial pyramidal, inhibitory interneurons, deep pyramidal) with two global (across-region) excitability parameters, modelled as self-inhibition: one for pyramidal (E) cells and one for inhibitory (I) cells, plus condition-specific modulations of each.

They fitted healthy EEG from B-SNIP and NAPLS-2 in three paradigms, each with a six-region network: paired clicks (n = 266), a passive double-deviant MMN oddball (n = 241) and an active P3b oddball (n = 237). Fits were good (r = 0.95, 0.89 and 0.84), E/I parameters were identifiable (all between-parameter |r| < 0.6), recovery of the E parameter was excellent and recovery of the I parameter only fair.

Simulating a loss of pyramidal excitability reduced paired-click N1/P2, MMN, P3a and P3b, the pattern seen in schizophrenia. Simulating a loss of I-cell excitability alone didn't change the paired-click waveform, and P3b showed E × I interactions. Changing a single parameter was enough to explain MMN changes, more parsimonious than earlier accounts needing many connectivity changes. The discussion suggests testing psychedelics, where 5-HT2A activation might be expected to increase pyramidal excitability.

CAVEATSThe patient-like effects are simulations; no patient data were fitted. I-cell recovery is only fair and test–retest reliability is unknown. The E self-inhibition parameter might partly reflect a PV interneuron loop rather than pyramidal cells themselves. Code and empirical priors are public (SPM12 r7771, MATLAB). No competing interests declared; funded by Adams's UKRI Future Leaders Fellowship and Wellcome (Friston). Version of record 27 Oct.
WHY IT
MATTERS
FOR YOU		Directly usable for your MMN DCM paper on gambling vs healthy controls: the empirical priors and global E/I parameters give a principled, low-dimensional comparison (one excitability parameter rather than many connections), and the same model could test whether psilocybin raises pyramidal excitability in your drug data. You could also check it against aperiodic-exponent E/I proxies in the same recordings.
REF / IDTransl Psychiatry, 31 Jul 2026 · doi:10.1038/s41398-026-04312-y · dcm_ei code + priors · Preprint
<PKND_002.02.02>
PREPRINTINTRACRANIAL EEGTRAVELLING WAVESICYMI

Travelling waves may hold working memory without overwriting it

Zeng, Hilla, Peylo, Taylor, Imbach, Sarnthein, Alamia & Sauseng (Zurich) · bioRxiv · posted 2 Oct 2026 (ICYMI)

In an artificial neural network simulation, transient travelling waves produced minimal synaptic plasticity, letting working memory be updated without interference.

In human intracranial recordings from the temporal lobe, waves started at encoding and persisted through maintenance and recall; on error trials there was no continuous wave activity.

CAVEATSPreprint; intracranial coverage is clinically determined, and sample sizes and effect sizes need checking in the full text.
WHY IT
MATTERS
FOR YOU		Travelling waves are one of your current analyses. Linking wave persistence to trial-level success is a template for asking whether drug-induced changes in wave direction or persistence matter behaviourally.
REF / IDbioRxiv, 2 Oct 2026 · doi:10.64898/2026.10.01.755588

03  Psychedelic neuroimaging
SEC.03  /  receptor occupancy meets blood flow
<PKND_002.03.01>
PEER REVIEWEDPET-MRILSDICYMI

LSD in simultaneous PET-MRI: high 5-HT2A occupancy, more cerebral blood flow, less global connectivity

McCulloch, … Fisher & Knudsen (Rigshospitalet, Copenhagen) · Nature Neuroscience · 12 Oct 2026 (ICYMI; covered by Medical Xpress 28 Oct)

Healthy volunteers, blinded to dose, had simultaneous PET-MRI after LSD. LSD occupied 5-HT2A receptors with high affinity, increased global cerebral blood flow and internal carotid flow, and decreased global functional connectivity; the connectivity decrease was negatively associated with the CBF change.

Subjective effects showed anticlockwise hysteresis against plasma levels. A separate psilocybin cohort (n = 25) showed the opposite pattern: lower CBF and higher global FC.

CAVEATSSmall sample for the LSD PET arm. The opposite LSD/psilocybin haemodynamic patterns complicate comparing BOLD effects across drugs.
WHY IT
MATTERS
FOR YOU		Another reason to treat psychedelic BOLD with care (following #001's haemodynamic delay paper), and the hysteresis result matters for when you sample EEG relative to plasma levels in acute sessions.
REF / IDNat Neurosci, 12 Oct 2026 · doi:10.1038/s41593-026-02462-2

05  Weekly explainer
SEC.05  /  what 'E/I balance' means at three levels
EXPLAINERINFO THEORY 101
 

Excitation/inhibition (E/I) balance is used at three levels that aren't the same thing. At the synapse, it's the ratio of excitatory to inhibitory currents a neuron receives. In a circuit model such as the canonical microcircuit, it's parameters like the gain or self-inhibition of pyramidal cells and interneurons. In scalp signals, it's usually inferred from proxies such as the aperiodic (1/f) exponent, where a steeper slope is read as relatively more inhibition.

Hauke et al.'s DCM sits in the middle: it estimates circuit parameters by fitting evoked responses, so the answer depends on the model's assumptions but is mechanistically interpretable. The aperiodic exponent is cheap and model-free but ambiguous, since many circuit changes can produce the same slope.

A good test is convergence: if a drug raises pyramidal excitability in the DCM, does the resting aperiodic exponent flatten in the same people? Agreement across levels is stronger evidence than either measure alone.

07  Brain–body coupling
SEC.07  /  brain, heart and gut
<PKND_002.07.01>
PEER REVIEWEDEEG + ECG + EGGOPEN DATAICYMI

A brain–heart–gut network that lives mostly at infra-slow timescales

Sitti, Pitti & Candia-Rivera · Journal of Physiology · 22 Oct 2026 (ICYMI)

Using open-access simultaneous resting EEG, ECG and EGG from 28 healthy participants, the authors related brain alpha power, cardiac sympathetic and parasympathetic indices and gastric rhythm power using the maximal information coefficient across time delays, with surrogate testing.

Interactions formed a multidirectional brain–heart–gut network, mainly at infra-slow timescales (0.01–0.1 Hz).

CAVEATSSmall sample at rest only; MIC detects dependence, not causation.
WHY IT
MATTERS
FOR YOU		The closest match yet to your BBC pipeline (EEG, EGG, ECG, respiration): same signals, an open dataset to test your code on, and a reminder to look below 0.1 Hz.
REF / IDJ Physiol, 22 Oct 2026 · doi:10.1113/JP291568

SEC.08  /  how temporal interference actually drives cortex
<PKND_002.08.01>
PREPRINTTWO-PHOTONTI

Temporal interference in mouse cortex: the kilohertz carrier alone activates neurons, and the envelope organises rhythm

Yoshimoto, Stern, Eggers, Acerbo, Laxpati, Berglund & Gutekunst (Emory) · bioRxiv · v2 posted 21 Oct 2026

Two-photon imaging in mice, immunohistochemistry and modelling showed that kilohertz stimulation without amplitude modulation evoked widespread activity. The amplitude modulation organised rhythmic responses at the modulation frequency, strongest near the interference centre.

CAVEATSPreprint in mice; field strengths and geometry that may not translate to human scalp TI.
WHY IT
MATTERS
FOR YOU		You plan to use TI in the cue-reactivity work: off-target kilohertz activation is a design concern, and sham conditions should include the carrier.
REF / IDbioRxiv, 21 Oct 2026 · doi:10.64898/2026.10.15.759497

09  Wildcards
SEC.09  /  consciousness, 15 times a second
<PKND_002.09.01>
PREPRINTNEUROPIXELSCONSCIOUSNESS

Conscious and suppressed images alternate about 15 times a second in the primate brain

Hesse, Lanfranchi, McGrory, Shi, Engel & Tsao (UC Berkeley) · bioRxiv · posted 16 Oct 2026

Neuropixels recordings from macaque IT face patches and prefrontal cortex during binocular flash suppression (validated behaviourally in 14 humans) showed activity alternating about 15 times a second between micro-epochs representing the conscious and the suppressed image; either could be reconstructed depending on the epoch, and feature tuning was amplified in conscious epochs.

CAVEATSPreprint, macaques; the link between micro-epochs and reported experience is inferred.
WHY IT
MATTERS
FOR YOU		Rhythmic sampling of conscious content is a natural target for psychedelic EEG: does the drug change the alternation rate or balance? Covered by Nature news (27 Oct) and #1 on the bioRxiv most-read tracker this week.
REF / IDbioRxiv, 16 Oct 2026 · doi:10.64898/2026.10.15.759299 · Nature news, 27 Oct

14  Best reviews of the week
SEC.14  /  syntheses worth your time
<PKND_002.14.01>
PEER REVIEWEDMETA-ANALYSISPSILOCYBINALCOHOLICYMI

Psilocybin for alcohol use disorder: a short-term signal that mostly fades

Florineth, … Soravia · Journal of Psychopharmacology · 8 Oct 2026 (ICYMI)

Six trials (N = 202; three RCTs). Short term, psilocybin reduced drinks per day (SMD 0.48, p = 0.004), % drinking days (0.50, p = 0.009) and % heavy drinking days (0.39, p = 0.020). At 4–28 weeks only % drinking days held (SMD 0.41, p = 0.032). Abstinence odds were higher (OR 2.78, p = 0.029). Adverse events were mostly mild.

CAVEATSFew, small, heterogeneous trials, including open-label ones; the authors call the evidence mixed.
WHY IT
MATTERS
FOR YOU		A sober baseline for psychedelic-addiction effect sizes, useful when powering your gambling and nicotine work.
REF / IDJ Psychopharmacol, 8 Oct 2026 · doi:10.1177/02698811261490221
