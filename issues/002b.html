SEC.01  /  neural field models of consciousness, fitted per patient
<PKND_002.01.01>
PEER REVIEWEDHD-EEGNEURAL FIELD MODELCONSCIOUSNESS

Fitting a corticothalamic neural field model to each patient's EEG separates minimally conscious from unresponsive states

Lín, Tewarie, … Laureys, Naccache, … Gosseries, Núñez & Sitt (multicentre) · PLoS Computational Biology · 28 Oct 2026

A biophysically informed corticothalamic neural field model was fitted to high-density EEG from two large independent datasets: 203 patients with unresponsive wakefulness syndrome (UWS), 270 in a minimally conscious state (MCS) and 74 healthy controls.

The fitted model captured spectral features across states of consciousness and showed reduced corticothalamic integrity in patients, more so in UWS than MCS, consistent with the mesocircuit hypothesis. EEG simulated per subject reproduced the empirical complexity patterns, and permutation entropy distinguished MCS from UWS in both real and simulated data.

CAVEATSSpectral fitting constrains corticothalamic parameters only indirectly, and classification performance needs checking in the full text. It drew attention on Hacker News (73 points), which is unusual for a modelling paper.
WHY IT
MATTERS
FOR YOU		This is whole-brain modelling of resting EEG done per subject at scale, close to what you want for drug and altered states. Fitting the same model before and after a psychedelic would let you ask whether complexity changes come from corticothalamic gain rather than cortex alone.
REF / IDPLoS Comput Biol, 28 Oct 2026 · doi:10.1371/journal.pcbi.1014905

02  EEG
SEC.02  /  your core modality: excitability, travelling waves, generators
<PKND_002.02.01>
REQUESTED BY YOUPEER REVIEWEDDCME/I

A canonical microcircuit DCM for estimating excitation/inhibition balance from ERPs

Hauke, Rodriguez-Sanchez, Oloye, Berndt, Pinotsis, Friston, Mathalon & Adams (UCL and others) · Translational Psychiatry · 31 Jul 2026 (open access)

Requested by you, so it's outside the usual one-week window. The authors extend the convolution-based canonical microcircuit (four populations: spiny stellate, superficial pyramidal, inhibitory interneurons, deep pyramidal) with two global (across-region) excitability parameters, modelled as self-inhibition: one for pyramidal (E) cells and one for inhibitory (I) cells, plus condition-specific modulations of each.

They fitted healthy EEG from B-SNIP and NAPLS-2 in three paradigms, each with a six-region network: paired clicks (n = 266), a passive double-deviant MMN oddball (n = 241) and an active P3b oddball (n = 237). Fits were good (r = 0.95, 0.89 and 0.84), E/I parameters were identifiable (all between-parameter |r| < 0.6), recovery of the E parameter was excellent and recovery of the I parameter only fair.

Simulating a loss of pyramidal excitability reduced paired-click N1/P2, MMN, P3a and P3b, the pattern seen in schizophrenia. Simulating a loss of I-cell excitability alone didn't change the paired-click waveform, and P3b showed E × I interactions. Changing a single parameter was enough to explain MMN changes, more parsimonious than earlier accounts needing many connectivity changes. The discussion suggests testing psychedelics, where 5-HT2A activation might be expected to increase pyramidal excitability.

CAVEATSThe patient-like effects are simulations; no patient data were fitted. I-cell recovery is only fair and test–retest reliability is unknown. The E self-inhibition parameter might partly reflect a PV interneuron loop rather than pyramidal cells themselves. Code and empirical priors are public (SPM12 r7771, MATLAB). No competing interests declared; funded by Adams's UKRI Future Leaders Fellowship and Wellcome (Friston). Version of record 27 Oct.
WHY IT
MATTERS
FOR YOU		Directly usable for your MMN DCM paper on gambling vs healthy controls: the empirical priors and global E/I parameters give a principled, low-dimensional comparison (one excitability parameter rather than many connections), and the same model could test whether psilocybin raises pyramidal excitability in your drug data. You could also check it against aperiodic-exponent E/I proxies in the same recordings.
REF / IDTransl Psychiatry, 31 Jul 2026 · doi:10.1038/s41398-026-04312-y · dcm_ei code + priors · Preprint
<PKND_002.02.02>
PEER REVIEWEDMEGEXCITABILITYAPERIODIC

'Cortical excitability' isn't one thing: six distinct dimensions in resting and 40 Hz MEG

Pellegrino, … Rasero · Communications Biology · 30 Oct 2026

In a large sample of healthy adults with whole-head MEG at rest and during 40 Hz auditory stimulation, the authors compared stimulus-driven synchronisation, spectral power, aperiodic (1/f) activity, signal complexity, long-range temporal correlations and phase–gamma synchronisation.

Hierarchical clustering found six distinct excitability dimensions with little redundancy. Aperiodic and alpha measures were strongly coupled and best aligned spatially with stimulus responses; temporal, complexity and synchronisation measures were largely independent. Each dimension related differently to cortical morphology and neurotransmitter receptor density.

CAVEATSHealthy adults only; sample size and the receptor-map method need checking in the full text, and receptor associations are spatial correlations across regions, not within people.
WHY IT
MATTERS
FOR YOU		If you report aperiodic exponent, LZ complexity and 40 Hz responses as 'excitability' in drug data, this says they're different constructs. Pre-register which dimension you mean.
REF / IDCommun Biol, 30 Oct 2026 · doi:10.1038/s42003-026-11005-1
<PKND_002.02.03>
PEER REVIEWEDEEGTRAVELLING WAVESOCD

Weaker forward theta travelling waves at rest in OCD

Perera, Bailey, Fitzgibbon & Fitzgerald · Psychiatry and Clinical Neurosciences · 31 Oct 2026

Resting (eyes closed) and task EEG (Go/NoGo, Flanker) in 25 people with OCD and 27 controls. Forward (posterior-to-anterior) and backward waves along midline electrodes were quantified with 2D FFTs in theta and alpha.

At rest there was a group × direction interaction in theta (P = 0.0006): weaker forward waves in OCD. During the Flanker task, forward theta increased more from rest in OCD (P = 0.022). There were no alpha or backward-wave differences and no links to symptom severity.

CAVEATSSmall groups, midline electrodes only, and no symptom association.
WHY IT
MATTERS
FOR YOU		The same 2D-FFT midline approach you can apply to drug EEG, with a clinical comparison group; a useful reference for addiction groups as well.
REF / IDPsychiatry Clin Neurosci, 31 Oct 2026 · doi:10.1111/pcn.70163
 
 EEG generators: what the skull does to cortical signals, measured directly (mice, 27 Oct). Mo … Song, Biosensors and Bioelectronics. Micro-ECoG and skull-screw EEG on one clock in awake mice (115 pairs, 3 mice): cortex-to-skull gain rose with frequency following a power law (skull EEG relatively preserves high frequencies) and shifted across task blocks. A calibration target for forward models and for interpreting aperiodic slopes. Tiny n. doi:10.1016/j.bios.2026.119370

SEC.03  /  primate psilocybin and ketamine entropy
<PKND_002.03.01>
PREPRINTMRI + MRSPSILOCYBINMACAQUE

A single psilocybin dose in macaques: network reorganisation, an anti-inflammatory CSF shift and raised prefrontal glutamate for a week

Wang, Chen, Sun, … · Research Square · posted 29 Oct 2026

Multimodal MRI, MRS, CSF proteomics and metabolomics, and single-nucleus RNA sequencing in cynomolgus macaques. Psilocybin increased frontoparietal integration with default-mode and limbic networks, transiently reduced its integration with somatomotor network and induced global segregation; some cortical, striatal and thalamic changes lasted a week.

CSF shifted from acute prostaglandin-dominated inflammation to a sustained omega-3-associated anti-inflammatory state, with sustained rises in dlPFC glutamate/glutamine and total creatine.

CAVEATSPreprint; animal numbers and dose need checking, and many modalities raise multiple-comparison questions.
WHY IT
MATTERS
FOR YOU		Primate network-level effects lasting a week give a post-acute window for your resting-state analyses, and MRS glutamate is an E/I-relevant marker to set beside EEG.
REF / IDResearch Square, 29 Oct 2026 · doi:10.21203/rs.3.rs-11081511/v1
 
 Low-dose ketamine raises BOLD entropy in treatment-resistant depression (J Affect Disord, 26 Oct). Liou … Chen. Two RCTs: sample entropy rose by day 3 in gyrus rectus, cerebellar vermis, substantia nigra, red nucleus and locus coeruleus (Trial 1) and in left amygdala (Trial 2, vs midazolam). A neuromodulatory-hub angle on entropy accounts. doi:10.1016/j.jad.2026.122673

04  Complexity and methods
SEC.04  /  what the aperiodic slope and DCM parameters can tell you
<PKND_002.04.01>
PEER REVIEWEDMODELLINGECoGAPERIODIC

The same E/I input shift gives different aperiodic slopes depending on circuit wiring

Cai, Chang, … Liu & Ming · International Journal of Neural Systems · 30 Oct 2026

The authors embedded electron-microscopy-reconstructed cortical microcircuits in near-critical dynamics and generated field potentials with fixed synaptic, membrane and forward-model parameters. The relationship between E/I input and the aperiodic exponent differed between biological and topology-randomised networks (a significant E/I × topology interaction).

In paired cortical ECoG and thalamic recordings from juvenile pigs under propofol, exponent changes were larger in thalamus than cortex, even after accounting for global state, baseline spectra and distance from criticality.

CAVEATSA model-plus-animal study; the pig recordings are under anaesthesia, and the claim is about interpretation, not a new estimator.
WHY IT
MATTERS
FOR YOU		A direct caution for reading slope changes under psychedelics as E/I shifts: the mapping depends on architecture, which differs across regions. It pairs with Hauke et al.'s model-based E/I estimates (see the explainer).
REF / IDInt J Neural Syst, 30 Oct 2026 · doi:10.1142/S012906572750033X
<PKND_002.04.02>
PREPRINTMEGDCM

DCM parameters sit between spectra and clinical scores for classifying individuals

Jafarian, … Rowe · bioRxiv · posted 28 Oct 2026

Resting MEG from 74 controls, 45 people with behavioural-variant frontotemporal dementia and 55 with progressive supranuclear palsy. Clinical scores classified individuals well (controls vs PSP accuracy 0.94), MEG spectral features weakly (~0.6) despite clear group differences, and DCM-inferred biophysical parameters in between (PSP vs controls 0.70; bvFTD vs PSP 0.74) while staying mechanistically interpretable.

CAVEATSPreprint; classification is used as an exploratory probe, and neurodegeneration is far from your populations.
WHY IT
MATTERS
FOR YOU		A realistic benchmark for your MMN DCM gambling paper: group differences in DCM parameters may not translate into individual-level separation, so frame results as mechanism first, biomarker second.
REF / IDbioRxiv, 28 Oct 2026 · doi:10.64898/2026.10.26.761866

05  Weekly explainer
SEC.05  /  what 'E/I balance' means at three levels
EXPLAINERINFO THEORY 101
 

Excitation/inhibition (E/I) balance is used at three levels that aren't the same thing. At the synapse, it's the ratio of excitatory to inhibitory currents a neuron receives. In a circuit model such as the canonical microcircuit, it's parameters like the gain or self-inhibition of pyramidal cells and interneurons. In scalp signals, it's usually inferred from proxies such as the aperiodic (1/f) exponent, where a steeper slope is read as relatively more inhibition.

This week makes the ambiguity concrete. Cai et al. (Complexity section) show that the same E/I input change gives different slope changes depending on circuit wiring, and Pellegrino et al. (EEG section) find 'excitability' splits into six partly independent MEG dimensions. Hauke et al.'s DCM instead estimates circuit parameters by fitting evoked responses: model-dependent but mechanistically interpretable.

A good test is convergence: if a drug raises pyramidal excitability in the DCM, does the resting aperiodic exponent flatten in the same people? Agreement across levels is stronger evidence than either measure alone.

SEC.06  /  neuroimaging and psychedelic-addiction
<PKND_002.06.01>
PEER REVIEWEDfMRIRCTOPIOIDS

Mindfulness-Oriented Recovery Enhancement changes prefrontal drug-cue responses in opioid use disorder

Huang, Ceceli, … Garland & Goldstein · JAMA Psychiatry · 28 Oct 2026

An RCT in inpatients with OUD: 8 weekly MORE sessions vs supportive therapy. In the cue-reactivity task (n = 59), MORE reduced drug-biased activity in middle frontal gyrus and precuneus when downregulating drug-cue responses relative to upregulating natural-reward responses.

MORE also reduced medial PFC synchronisation to drug scenes in a movie (n = 37) and extended abstinence at about 4 months (χ² = 6.7, P = .04; Cohen w = 0.19).

CAVEATSImaging subsets are small and unequal (24 vs 35), and the abstinence effect is modest.
WHY IT
MATTERS
FOR YOU		A clean template for your cue-reactivity work: a drug vs natural-reward regulation contrast plus naturalistic movie synchrony, both usable pre/post psilocybin in gambling or nicotine.
REF / IDJAMA Psychiatry, 28 Oct 2026 · doi:10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2026.3388
 
 Psilocybin, cognitive stability and drinking in AUD with depression (medRxiv, 29 Oct). Sayalı … Barrett. Secondary analysis of an RCT (12 psilocybin, 13 placebo): psilocybin slowed cued task switching, but most of 34 cognitive measures were inconclusive and none survived FDR. Drinking fell in both arms, and shifts toward stability tracked drinking reduction. Small and exploratory. doi:10.64898/2026.10.27.26366630
 
 MDMA-assisted therapy for veterans with PTSD and alcohol use disorder (Alcohol, 30 Oct). Eaton … Haass-Koffler. First open-label pilot (N = 10): PTSD symptoms fell (d = 1.24); drinks per drinking day fell 31% and heavy drinking days 54%, not clinically significant; no serious adverse events. doi:10.1016/j.alcohol.2026.107464

07  Brain–body coupling
SEC.07  /  breath, heart and the cortex
<PKND_002.07.01>
PEER REVIEWEDEEGRESPIRATIONSLEEP

Quiet breathing evokes scalp EEG potentials in wakefulness and in every sleep stage

Grassion, Andrillon, … Similowski · Journal of Physiology · 28 Oct 2026

Cleveland Family Study polysomnography from 729 participants (median 7,871 respiratory cycles each): a pre-inspiratory component about 300 ms before inspiration and post-inspiratory peaks up to 3 s after, persisting in N2, N3 and REM with a simplified K-complex-like shape and more spindle-range activity. Present regardless of BMI and in the 203 participants without sleep-disordered breathing.

CAVEATSPhysiological and clinical significance unknown; sparse polysomnography montage.
WHY IT
MATTERS
FOR YOU		Respiration-locked averaging is an easy first analysis for your BBC pipeline, and a respiratory 'evoked' term to account for before attributing EEG changes to cortex alone.
REF / IDJ Physiol, 28 Oct 2026 · doi:10.1113/JP290737
 
 Brain–body timing during emotional videos (MEG + physiology preprint, 29 Oct). Xu … Parviainen. 92 participants, MEG plus cardiac, electrodermal, respiratory and facial EMG. Corrugator activity preceded matching neural patterns by about 1 s, while heart rate and electrodermal activity followed by about 1.7 s and 4.5 s; respiration rate didn't exceed the decoupled reference. doi:10.64898/2026.10.28.762567

SEC.08  /  learning under uncertainty
<PKND_002.08.01>
REVIEWED PREPRINTSINGLE UNITSPSILOCYBIN

Psilocybin tilts learning toward rewarded outcomes in rat prefrontal cortex

Jacobs, Torrado Pacheco, Olson, Langdon & Moghaddam · eLife reviewed preprint · 30 Oct 2026

In a value-based probabilistic choice task with computational modelling and mPFC single-unit recordings, psilocybin improved choice under uncertainty and shifted learning rates bidirectionally: more updating from rewarded actions, less from unrewarded ones. Neural encoding of rewarded outcomes strengthened and of unrewarded outcomes weakened.

CAVEATSRodents; acute exposure; an eLife reviewed preprint with public reviews, not a final version of record.
WHY IT
MATTERS
FOR YOU		A mechanistic prediction for gambling disorder: asymmetric learning from wins vs losses under psilocybin is testable with model-based EEG (e.g. FRN coding of positive vs negative prediction errors).
REF / IDeLife RP, 30 Oct 2026 · doi:10.7554/eLife.112830.1

SEC.16  /  syntheses worth your time
<PKND_002.16.01>
PEER REVIEWEDMETA-ANALYSISLSD

LSD for depressive symptoms: a meta-analysis of five RCTs

Chiorean, Kim, Lustig & Husain · Journal of Psychopharmacology · 30 Oct 2026

Five RCTs (N = 227), preregistered: LSD reduced depressive symptoms vs comparators (adjusted SMD −0.65, 95% CI −0.92 to −0.38), with no significant heterogeneity and three studies at low risk of bias. Severe adverse events possibly related to study parameters included three hospitalisations.

CAVEATSDepression was mostly a secondary outcome in anxiety trials, and designs varied (three with psychological support).
WHY IT
MATTERS
FOR YOU		Useful context for LSD-related EEG work and for discussion sections on effect sizes.
REF / IDJ Psychopharmacol, 30 Oct 2026 · doi:10.1177/02698811261488460
 
 Psychedelics and psychosis, 1984–2025: a systematic review (J Psychopharmacol, 27 Oct). Silva Reis … Dos Santos. 52 papers: no consistent population-level link; one transient case in controlled trials; risk factors are personal or family history and unsupervised use. doi:10.1177/02698811261488575
 
 Psychedelics for addiction: treatment or a 'learning window'? (Brain Sciences, 29 Oct). Buciuc, Gold & Fuehrlein. Compound-by-compound review; the strongest human signals are psilocybin in tobacco and alcohol use disorders, though the alcohol signal rests on two small, discordant RCTs. doi:10.3390/brainsci16111184
