Hi Patrick, this covers Monday 2 to Sunday 8 November 2026. It's the first single, merged issue since the A/B test: everything below was found by web search, the database pipeline or both, then checked against its source.

The thread this week is plasticity claims meeting harder tests. A placebo-controlled PET study in depression finds psilocybin's antidepressant effect arrives without a group-level rise in the synaptic marker SV2A, five mouse labs running identical protocols find that psilocybin's acute effects replicate but most of its 24-hour 'antidepressant-like' effects don't, and two papers disagree about whether Gi signalling separates hallucinogenic from non-hallucinogenic 5-HT2A agonists. On the methods side there's a DCM that runs from cortex through brainstem to the heart, and an EEG study showing travelling waves take on the rhythm of whichever sense is cueing attention.

Online chatter was quiet this week: none of the pipeline's top papers drew more than a single Bluesky post. The final section is new: Catch-up, five must-read papers from the last decade or so, one per interest area.

01  The headline act
SEC.01  /  psilocybin's antidepressant effect, without a measurable synapse boost
<PKND_003.01.01>
PREPRINTSV2A PETRCT SUB-STUDYPSILOCYBINMDD

Psilocybin's rapid antidepressant effect came without a group-level rise in SV2A, but baseline SV2A predicted who improved

Yngwe, Plavén-Sigray, Andersson … Tiger & Lundberg (Karolinska Institutet) · medRxiv · posted 8 Nov 2026

A pre-registered [11C]UCB-J PET sub-study of a published randomised trial. Thirty-five people with moderate-to-severe recurrent major depression were randomised 1:1 to a single 25 mg psilocybin dose or 100 mg niacin as active placebo, both with psychotherapeutic support. PET ran 2–7 days before and 15 (±7) days after dosing, quantifying SV2A (a synaptic vesicle protein used as a proxy for synaptic density) in hippocampus and frontal cortex.

Despite significant clinical improvement in the psilocybin group, there was no evidence of a group-level SV2A increase relative to niacin (VT or BPND). In secondary analyses, higher baseline VT went with greater later improvement after psilocybin, surviving FDR correction at Day 8 and Day 15 (two of three pre-registered timepoints). Associations between SV2A change and improvement appeared in unadjusted analyses but weakened after adjusting for baseline VT and MADRS and didn't survive FDR correction.

CAVEATSPreprint. Small PET sample (n = 35 across two arms). SV2A is an indirect marker of synaptic density, and PET resolution can't see local or cell-type-specific remodelling; the scan was about two weeks after dosing, so a transient change earlier on can't be ruled out. The baseline-predicts-response finding is secondary and needs replication.
WHY IT
MATTERS
FOR YOU		This is the human test of the 'psychedelics grow synapses' story that much of the plasticity literature leans on, and it comes back null at group level. For your pre/post psilocybin EEG work it's a reason to frame post-acute changes as functional (e.g. plasticity-sensitive EEG measures such as evoked-response potentiation or aperiodic shifts) rather than assuming structural change, and the baseline-predicts-response result is the kind of treatment-outcome predictor you'd want to test against baseline EEG features.
REF / IDmedRxiv, 8 Nov 2026 · doi:10.64898/2026.11.06.26367247
 
 Read it alongside (ICYMI, Translational Psychiatry, 15 Jul; covered by PsyPost 3 Nov): Johansen, … Knudsen (Copenhagen) gave 15 healthy volunteers 0.3 mg/kg psilocybin with [11C]UCB-J PET at baseline and one week. The open-label, single-arm study stopped for futility at interim (mean change about 2–3%, not significant). Exploratory: people dosed in a therapy-like room rather than an MRI scanner had more intense mystical-type experiences and larger SV2A increases, but setting wasn't randomised (the first five were scanned). Two independent groups, the same tracer, no robust group-level effect. doi:10.1038/s41398-026-04285-y

02  EEG
SEC.02  /  travelling waves, MMN at single-subject level, aperiodic shifts
<PKND_003.02.01>
PREPRINTEEGTRAVELLING WAVESATTENTION

Attention travels as a wave at the rhythm of whichever sense is cueing it

Kong, Petras, Alexander, Rohaut & Dugué (Université Paris Cité) · bioRxiv · posted 5 Nov 2026

Alpha lateralisation is a standard marker of sustained visuospatial attention, and anterior-to-posterior alpha travelling waves had recently been shown to lateralise too. The question here: are the waves top-down attention signals or a by-product of attending to visual input?

The authors cued attention through different senses (auditory cueing vs visual stimulation) while keeping a visual discrimination task. With auditory cueing, lateralised travelling waves appeared in theta; with visual stimulation, in alpha. Wave lateralisation at both frequencies correlated trial by trial with alpha power lateralisation, which the authors read as attention signals propagating at the intrinsic rhythm of the engaged sensory system.

CAVEATSPreprint; sample size and wave-detection details need checking in the full text. Scalp travelling-wave estimates are sensitive to volume conduction and reference choice.
WHY IT
MATTERS
FOR YOU		Travelling waves are one of your current analyses. The design is a neat template: manipulate what drives the wave and see whether its frequency follows. Under psychedelics, where forward/backward wave balance reportedly shifts, the same logic could ask whether drug-induced wave changes follow the dominant sensory input (e.g. music vs eyes-closed rest).
REF / IDbioRxiv, 5 Nov 2026 · doi:10.64898/2026.11.04.763906
<PKND_003.02.02>
PREPRINTEEGERP / MMNDISORDERS OF CONSCIOUSNESS

How often can you actually see an MMN in one person? A benchmark in healthy controls and minimally conscious patients

Pavlov, Boltzmann, Jenner … Rollnik & Kotchoubey (Alabama-Birmingham, Tübingen) · medRxiv · posted 2 Nov 2026

48 healthy controls and 26 patients in a minimally conscious state (MCS) completed six auditory paradigms: multifeature MMN, passive and active oddball P300, own-name, semantic oddball and sentence N400. Effects were classified with blinded expert visual scoring, ROI ANOVA, cluster-based permutation and SVM decoding.

In controls, visual scoring found an MMN to at least one deviant and a P300 in both oddball tasks in all 48; task-related P300 enhancement (command following) was detected in 81%, sentence-congruity effects in 69% and the own-name effect in only 35%. Patients showed lower detection: MMN in 27% for the short-duration deviant and 65% when any of five deviant types counted. Prognostic associations were tested in the 22 surviving patients.

CAVEATSPreprint; modest patient sample, and detection rates depend on which classification method you choose.
WHY IT
MATTERS
FOR YOU		For your gambling vs control MMN DCM paper, this is a useful reference on single-subject MMN reliability: it shows how much detectability depends on deviant type and method, which matters if you fit DCMs per participant and need to justify that each person has a measurable response.
REF / IDmedRxiv, 2 Nov 2026 · doi:10.64898/2026.10.31.26366991
 
 Walking flattens the aperiodic exponent and delays the auditory N1 (preprint, 6 Nov). Chen, Chen, Zhang & Cao (Zhejiang). Mobile EEG in 30 adults: walking delayed N1 latency (< 10 ms), with a scalp-wide decrease in aperiodic exponent, and larger exponent drops went with larger N1 delays. A reminder that state (posture, movement) shifts the exponent you might read as E/I. doi:10.64898/2026.11.04.764442
 
 Aperiodic exponent as a stimulation-response predictor (NeuroImage, 2 Nov). van Bueren, … Cohen Kadosh. tRNS sped learning vs sham without reliably shifting resting exponent or fNIRS HbO; higher resting exponent and lower resting HbO went with larger gains, but the EEG moderation effects weakened with participant-specific slopes, and the authors call for replication. Relevant background for predicting TI responses from baseline EEG. doi:10.1016/j.neuroimage.2026.122329
 
 Most-read neuro preprint pick (#21 in October on the bioRxiv tracker): sustained attention, alpha and the 1/f slope. Barne, Fu & Lavie (UCL). EEG during a continuous performance task under low vs high perceptual load: time-on-task increased alpha power widely and steepened the 1/f slope in a left temporoparietal cluster, alongside worse detection and more mind wandering. A tidy within-session example of periodic and aperiodic changes moving together. doi:10.64898/2026.08.17.745232 (The October #1, the rapid-alternation consciousness preprint, was in #002.)

03  Complexity and methods
SEC.03  /  what couples oscillations to the aperiodic slope, and a human-tuned HNN
<PKND_003.03.01>
PREPRINTINTRACRANIAL EEGAPERIODICMODELLING

Oscillations and the aperiodic exponent are coupled only sometimes, and local inhibition decides when

Lu, Li, Mao … Xiao & Tian (NYU Shanghai, East China Normal University) · bioRxiv · posted 3 Nov 2026

Bias-reduced analyses of 3,245 intracranial contacts from 61 awake people found significant coupling between oscillatory frequency and aperiodic exponent in fewer than 13% of contacts per band.

A connectome-constrained dynamical model predicted that neither changing input to a local circuit nor removing long-range connections would modulate the coupling, whereas stronger local inhibitory feedback would make frequency and exponent covary. Three experiments supported this: transcranial stimulation shifted each component independently, focal ablation of remote tissue didn't change coupling, and coupling emerged during seizures, which alter local E/I balance.

CAVEATSPreprint; epilepsy-patient recordings, and the seizure test is a strong perturbation rather than a graded one.
WHY IT
MATTERS
FOR YOU		Directly relevant to your aperiodic and waveform work: it argues periodic and aperiodic parameters are usually separable, and that their coupling could itself be a state marker of local inhibition. Worth checking in your psychedelic EEG whether peak frequency and exponent start to covary under the drug.
REF / IDbioRxiv, 3 Nov 2026 · doi:10.64898/2026.10.29.762761
<PKND_003.03.02>
PREPRINTEEG/MEG GENERATORSBIOPHYSICAL MODELOPEN SOFTWARE

The Human Neocortical Neurosolver gets a human-tuned cortical column with dendritic spikes

Duecker, Soplata, Tolley … Daniels & Jones (Brown University) · bioRxiv · posted 6 Nov 2026

HNN's canonical neocortical circuit (pyramidal cells and interneurons in L2/3 and L5) was originally built from rodent and non-human primate data. This update tunes electrophysiological properties and synaptic connections to human tissue recordings, and adds dendritic Ca2+ spikes in L5 extratelencephalic-projecting neurons and shorter dendritic spikes in L2/3 pyramidal cells.

The authors show how the model can separate somatic, synaptic and dendritic contributions to E/MEG signatures, and ship workflows and tutorials for simulating event-related responses and oscillations and recording membrane potentials, Ca2+ dynamics and synaptic currents.

CAVEATSPreprint; a model update, so its value depends on validation against your kind of data.
WHY IT
MATTERS
FOR YOU		A bridge between your ERP DCM work and cellular mechanisms: HNN simulates ERPs and rhythms from a laminar circuit, so it's a way to ask what an MMN or drug-altered ERP might mean at the level of L5 dendrites, where 5-HT2A receptors are dense. It also links to this week's Catch-up on where EEG comes from.
REF / IDbioRxiv, 6 Nov 2026 · doi:10.64898/2026.10.29.762964 · hnn-core on GitHub
 
 A whole-brain model with several receptor maps at once (Frontiers Comput Neurosci, 4 Nov). Monge-Asensio, … Kringelbach & Deco. Pharmacological resting fMRI under atomoxetine plus a whole-brain model carrying adrenergic and dopaminergic receptor maps. Atomoxetine stabilised FC dynamics within a posterior catecholaminergic network; individual receptor parameters didn't differ, but their collective weighting converged towards empirical binding affinities. A template for multi-receptor models of drugs that hit more than 5-HT2A (LSD, for instance). doi:10.3389/fncom.2026.1854866
 
 Entropy in glucose, not just BOLD (preprint, 3 Nov). Deery, … Egan & Jamadar (Monash). Functional [18F]FDG-PET with simultaneous fMRI in 84 adults: metabolic multiscale entropy went with network metastability, cortical thickness, CMRglc and CBF, and was lower in older adults; cognition links were largely explained by age. A metabolic angle on the entropic-brain measures you use. doi:10.64898/2026.11.02.763776

04  Weekly explainer
SEC.04  /  conditional mutual information on ordinal patterns, for brain–heart direction
EXPLAINERINFO THEORY 101
 

Mutual information (MI) asks how much knowing one signal reduces your uncertainty about another, in bits, without assuming the relationship is linear. Conditional MI asks the same question while holding a third variable fixed. To get direction, you ask: does the past of the heart signal tell me about the future of the EEG beyond what the EEG's own past already tells me? That conditional quantity is the core of transfer entropy, and if it's larger heart→brain than brain→heart, you infer a dominant direction.

The permutation part (Saibene et al., Brain–body section) is a way to make this estimable on short, noisy data. Each short window of a signal is replaced by its ordinal pattern, the rank order of its values (e.g. 'up, up, down'), and probabilities are computed over these symbols instead of raw amplitudes. It's the same trick behind permutation entropy: robust to amplitude scaling and slow drift, cheap to compute, but it throws away amplitude information, and results depend on the embedding dimension and delay you choose.

Two cautions for your pipeline: a directed information measure is still 'predictive' rather than causal, and shared drivers (respiration driving both heart rate and EEG, for instance) can create apparent direction. Conditioning on respiration, or comparing with a model-based approach like this week's Embodied DCM, is the stronger test.

05  Addiction
SEC.05  /  cue reactivity, neuroimaging only
<PKND_003.05.01>
PEER REVIEWEDfMRICUE REACTIVITYOPIOID USE DISORDER

In opioid use disorder, blunted responses to aversive cues stood out more than drug-cue reactivity

Okan, Kuplicki, Davis … Sinha & Stewart (Laureate Institute for Brain Research and others) · Biol Psychiatry CNNI · 3 Nov 2026

People with OUD (n = 64) and controls (n = 35) viewed negative, neutral and drug cues during fMRI and rated valence, arousal, stress and urge; OUD participants were followed for three months (29 abstinent, 35 relapsed).

OUD participants reported more arousal, stress and urge to drug cues, but showed lower nucleus accumbens, insula and ACC responses to negative cues; whole-brain analyses also found blunted inferior frontal and precentral responses to negative cues and blunted orbitofrontal responses to drug cues. Abstinent and relapsed subgroups didn't differ on ratings or brain responses.

CAVEATSThe relapse comparison is small and null; the authors call for replication with samples powered for longitudinal outcomes.
WHY IT
MATTERS
FOR YOU		Relevant to your gambling and nicotine cue-reactivity designs: including an aversive-cue condition, not just drug vs neutral, picked up the group difference here. Worth considering for your EEG cue-reactivity paradigm, and as a contrast to try before and after TI.
REF / IDBiol Psychiatry CNNI, 3 Nov 2026 · doi:10.1016/j.bpsc.2026.10.014
 
 Alcohol cues, skin conductance and the pgACC, by sex (Addiction Biology, 3 Nov). Chen, … Li (Yale). 94 adult drinkers, fMRI with skin conductance during alcohol vs neutral cues. Females had larger cue SCRs, but SCR tracked AUDIT scores only in males, as did a pgACC cluster whose cue response correlated with SCR. A small example of adding a peripheral arousal channel to cue reactivity, close to your brain–body set-up. doi:10.1111/adb.70197

06  Brain–body coupling
SEC.06  /  from cortex through brainstem to the heart, and breathing-locked EEG
<PKND_003.06.01>
PREPRINTDCMEEG + ECGBRAINSTEM MODEL

Embodied DCM: one generative model from cortex through brainstem to the sinoatrial node

Levy, Zeidman, Allen & Friston (UCL Imaging Neuroscience) · bioRxiv · posted 3 Nov 2026

The authors extend dynamic causal modelling to the brain–body axis. Their Brainstem Cardiac Network is a closed-loop neural mass model of five brainstem nuclei, a viscerosensory thalamic relay and sinoatrial node dynamics, so directed effective connectivity can be estimated across the cortical–brainstem–cardiac hierarchy.

As an illustrative case study, they jointly inverted simultaneous EEG and ECG from an auditory Go/No-Go task with a multimodal observation model, and showed the framework can arbitrate between competing hypotheses about cortical cytoarchitecture (granular vs agranular microcircuits).

CAVEATSPreprint and a methods/framework paper: the case study is illustrative, not a validation across many participants. Brainstem nuclei are latent states inferred through the model, not measured.
WHY IT
MATTERS
FOR YOU		This sits exactly where two of your analyses meet: DCM of ERPs and the EEG/ECG/EGG/respiration coupling pipeline. It offers a mechanistic alternative to coupling indices, for example asking whether psilocybin changes cortex→heart effective connectivity, and it runs in the SPM/DCM ecosystem you already use for the MMN paper.
REF / IDbioRxiv, 3 Nov 2026 · doi:10.64898/2026.10.27.762241
<PKND_003.06.02>
PEER REVIEWEDEEG + RESPIRATIONOLFACTIONPNAS

Analysing EEG by breath phase rather than clock time reveals odour responses that time-locking hides

Hathaway & Coleman (Stanford) · PNAS · 6 Nov 2026

Simultaneous nasal respiration and EEG in 17 participants, with naturalistic odours (peppermint, oregano, grapefruit) delivered in sync with each person's breathing. Odours evoked respiratory phase-locked event-related spectral perturbations in frontal and temporal regions during the first breath after onset: theta during inhalation and alpha during exhalation were most discriminative, patterns obscured in standard time-locked analyses because breath duration varies.

Leave-one-subject-out classification detected odours above chance (AUC 0.79 peppermint, 0.67 oregano, 0.62 grapefruit).

CAVEATSSmall sample (n = 17) and an olfactory task; modest AUCs for two of three odours.
WHY IT
MATTERS
FOR YOU		A methods idea you can lift straight into your brain–body pipeline: re-express EEG on a respiratory-phase axis (warping each breath to a common cycle) before averaging. It pairs with #002's breathing-evoked EEG potentials, and could help separate respiration-locked activity from drug effects in your acute psychedelic recordings.
REF / IDPNAS, 6 Nov 2026 · doi:10.1073/pnas.2619920123
 
 Directional brain–heart interaction with permutation conditional MI (Frontiers Netw Physiol, 6 Nov). Saibene, … Puthusserypady (DTU). Validated on synthetic coupled series, then applied to EEG + ECG in 36 people: brain–heart interaction complexity rose during mental arithmetic vs baseline, over anterior and posterior regions and both vagal and sympathovagal HRV components. See this week's explainer. doi:10.3389/fnetp.2026.1930070
 
 HRV biofeedback through a spectral DCM lens (Appl Psychophysiol Biofeedback, 3 Nov). Ma, … Moeller. Secondary analysis of an open dataset: 8 weeks of HRV biofeedback (n = 15) vs a video-game control (n = 17), resting fMRI with spectral DCM and PEB. Biofeedback increased mPFC→ACC and mPFC→thalamus effective connectivity and reduced right hippocampus→VLPFC, the latter linked to SDNN change. Small groups, but a worked example of DCM-PEB on a breathing intervention. doi:10.1007/s10484-026-09818-1

SEC.07  /  psychedelic circuits in mice
<PKND_003.07.01>
REVIEWED PREPRINTWIDEFIELD CALCIUMDOIMOUSE

DOI makes the mouse cortex less modular, via thalamo-cortical and long-range cortical influence

Doronin, Stroukov & Keller (Friedrich Miescher Institute, Basel) · eLife reviewed preprint · 2 Nov 2026

Widefield calcium imaging in awake mice: the psychedelic DOI, in a 5-HT2A-dependent way, acutely reduced cortical network modularity by increasing correlations between anterolateral and posteromedial cortical domains. Functional influence measurements showed increased thalamo-cortical and long-range cortico-cortical influence onto the anterolateral domain, which the authors speculate drives the increased coupling.

CAVEATSMouse, one drug (DOI), acute only; the causal link from influence to coupling is described as speculation. eLife reviewed preprints carry an editorial assessment rather than an accept/reject decision.
WHY IT
MATTERS
FOR YOU		A circuit-level counterpart of the reduced modularity and network integration seen in human psychedelic fMRI, pointing to thalamo-cortical input, which is central to thalamic-gating (CSTC) accounts of psychedelic action.
REF / IDeLife reviewed preprint, 2 Nov 2026 · doi:10.7554/eLife.112732.1
 
 Psilocybin and nucleus accumbens inhibition via astrocytes (Exp Mol Med, 5 Nov). Kang, … Koo. In rodents, psilocybin-like tryptamines raised NAc activity while lowering extracellular GABA; psilocin reduced tonic (not miniature) GABA currents in medium spiny neurons; psilocybin lowered astrocytic GABA and raised astrocytic NFATc4/ALDH5A1, with 5-HT2A antagonism blunting astrocyte calcium responses. Mesolimbic tone is relevant to the reward circuitry in your addiction work. doi:10.1038/s12276-026-01872-3

08  Critiques and nulls
SEC.08  /  replication at scale, and a signalling dispute
<PKND_003.08.01>
PEER REVIEWEDMULTI-LAB REPLICATIONPSILOCYBINMOUSE

Five labs, one protocol: psilocybin's acute effects in mice replicate, most 24-hour 'antidepressant-like' effects don't

Lu, White, Raymond … Heifets & Malenka (Stanford-led, five labs) · Nature Neuroscience · 2 Nov 2026

Five independent laboratories ran the same experiments on psilocybin (2 mg/kg i.p.), using about 200 male and female mice per experiment.

Psilocybin robustly and replicably increased anxiety- and avoidance-related behaviour and decreased fear expression acutely. But at 24 hours it did not replicably reduce anxiety- or depression-like behaviour or facilitate fear extinction, and it didn't alter social preference or social reward learning. The authors conclude that some reported persistent effects in mice may be modest and/or inconsistent.

CAVEATSOne dose and route, and a specific behavioural battery; null replication of behaviour doesn't speak directly to structural plasticity or to human efficacy.
WHY IT
MATTERS
FOR YOU		Together with the headline SV2A null, this is a strong week for calibrating how much of the persistent-effects literature to lean on when you motivate post-acute EEG hypotheses. Cite multi-lab results like this when reviewers ask why your post-acute predictions are cautious.
REF / IDNat Neurosci, 2 Nov 2026 · doi:10.1038/s41593-026-02500-z
 
 Does Gi signalling separate hallucinogenic from non-hallucinogenic 5-HT2A agonists? Two papers disagree. Miranda-Azpiazu, … González-Maeso & Meana (Neurochem Int, 6 Nov), in post-mortem human prefrontal cortex: DOI, TCB-2 and LSD activated Gq/11 and Gi1 via 5-HT2A, while non-hallucinogenic Ariadne, 2-Br-LSD and lisuride activated Gq/11 but not Gi1; they note a larger drug panel is needed. doi:10.1016/j.neuint.2026.106285 In the other corner (ICYMI, ACS Chem Neurosci, 17 Oct), Hatzipantelis & Olson reanalysed source data from an earlier Gi paper by Xu et al. and argue Gi activation is less predictive of hallucinogenic potential than canonical 5-HT2A effectors. doi:10.1021/acschemneuro.6c00743 Unresolved, and relevant to how non-hallucinogenic 'psychoplastogens' are defined.

 Meditative 'cessation' at 7T: consciousness switched off without global suppression (Imaging Neuroscience, 5 Nov). Yang, … Sacchet (MGH). <PKND_003.13.01>

A multilevel E/I framework for how psychedelics change affect and the self

Winkelman (Arizona State) · Journal of Psychedelic Studies · 6 Nov 2026

A theoretical article proposing that 5-HT2A-driven shifts in excitation–inhibition balance propagate across a four-level hierarchy (homeostatic–autonomic, limbic, salience network, cortical self-referential), with post-acute plasticity and 'inhibitory recalibration' stabilising altered affective dynamics.

CAVEATSSingle-author narrative synthesis, not a systematic review; the framework is offered as testable, but the evidence is organised narratively.
WHY IT
MATTERS
FOR YOU		It ties E/I, interoception and self-processing together, exactly the triangle your EEG aperiodic, DCM E/I and brain–body analyses could test. Use it as a hypothesis generator rather than evidence.

<PKND_003.13.02>
PEER REVIEWEDNARRATIVE REVIEW5-MeO-DMTTRD

The clinical pharmacology of mebufotenin (5-MeO-DMT) for treatment-resistant depression

Likić, … Jacobs & Groeneveld (Zagreb, CHDR Leiden) · CNS Drugs · 4 Nov 2026

A narrative review built on a structured search of PubMed/MEDLINE, EMBASE, Scopus, ClinicalTrials.gov and grey literature to 15 Aug 2026, plus regulatory and sponsor disclosures. It covers mebufotenin's atypical pharmacology: a non-selective serotonin agonist with high 5-HT1A affinity (Ki 3.0 nM) and markedly lower 5-HT2A affinity, with reported selectivity of roughly 30- to 300-fold depending on the assay.

CAVEATSNarrative rather than systematic synthesis; several authors are affiliated with the Centre for Human Drug Research (Leiden), so check their declared interests.

PEER REVIEWED2018REVIEWTRAVELLING WAVES

Cortical travelling waves: mechanisms and computational principles

Muller, Chavane, Reynolds & Sejnowski · Nature Reviews Neuroscience · 2018

The review that pulled together evidence for travelling waves at mesoscopic (single-area) and macroscopic (whole-brain) scales, and placed it within theory of how recurrent networks generate and propagate waves. It argues waves transiently modulate spiking and excitability as they pass and may serve functions from memory consolidation in sleep to processing dynamic visual input in the awake cortex.

Why it mattered: it gave the field a shared vocabulary and a mechanistic framing (waves as a computational feature, not just a phase artefact), which much of the later EEG/MEG travelling-wave literature builds on.

CAVEATSA review, mostly built on invasive animal recordings; it says less about how to measure waves robustly at the scalp.
WHY IT
MATTERS
FOR YOU		It's the theoretical backbone for your travelling-wave analyses, and useful for deciding what a psychedelic-induced change in wave direction or speed would mean mechanistically.
SINCE THENDavis, Muller et al. (Nature, 2020) showed spontaneous travelling waves in awake marmoset visual cortex predicted target detection, moving waves from anaesthesia into behaving perception. doi:10.1038/s41586-020-2802-y
READING LEVELWorth a full read
REF / IDNat Rev Neurosci, 2018 · doi:10.1038/nrn.2018.20
<PKND_003.14.02>
PEER REVIEWED2017OPINIONEEG GENERATORS

Where does EEG come from and what does it mean?

Cohen · Trends in Neurosciences · 2017

A short, provocative opinion piece arguing that we know 'shockingly little' about where EEG signals come from and what they mean. It sets out what is known, why the answers matter, and how modern circuit tools (measuring and manipulating neurons with high spatiotemporal precision) might finally provide answers, proposing oscillations as the best anchor because they're seen across scales and species.

Why it mattered: it became a widely cited framing for the generators problem and a call to link scalp EEG to circuit mechanisms.

CAVEATSAn opinion piece, not new data; much of the cross-scale work it called for has appeared since.
WHY IT
MATTERS
FOR YOU		Core interest 7. It's a good lens for interpreting psychedelic EEG changes (alpha loss, aperiodic flattening) without over-reading them as specific cellular events, and it pairs with this week's HNN update.
SINCE THENThe Human Neocortical Neurosolver (Neymotin et al., eLife 2020) turned a laminar circuit model of EEG/MEG generators into open software; this week's preprint updates it with a human-tuned circuit. doi:10.7554/eLife.51214
READING LEVELSkim the abstract
CODE/DATANot for this paper, but HNN is open: hnn-core
REF / IDTrends Neurosci, 2017 · doi:10.1016/j.tins.2017.02.004
<PKND_003.14.03>
PEER REVIEWED2018fMRI + EGGBRAIN–BODY

Stomach–brain synchrony reveals a delayed-connectivity resting-state network

Rebollo, Devauchelle, Béranger & Tallon-Baudry (ENS Paris) · eLife · 2018

Using simultaneous resting fMRI and electrogastrography, the authors found a 'gastric network': brain regions phase-synchronised with the stomach's slow (~0.05 Hz) electrical rhythm. It cuts across classical resting-state networks, partly overlaps autonomic regions, and is dominated by areas that map bodily and external space (somatomotor cortices through to the extrastriate body area and dorsal precuneus), with a precise temporal sequence within each gastric cycle.

Why it mattered: it showed that resting-state organisation isn't only about instantaneous connectivity, and put the stomach on the map as a driver of slow brain dynamics.

CAVEATSHealthy participants only; EGG is noisy, and the phase-locking approach needs careful surrogate statistics.
WHY IT
MATTERS
FOR YOU		The founding paper for the gastric part of your EEG/EGG/ECG/respiration pipeline, and directly relevant to psychedelic fMRI, where slow BOLD changes could partly reflect visceral rhythms.
SINCE THENWolpert, Rebollo & Tallon-Baudry (Psychophysiology, 2020) published a practical EGG recording and analysis pipeline with MATLAB code and normative data from 117 people. doi:10.1111/psyp.13599
READING LEVELRead the methods
CODE/DATAAnalysis scripts: stomach_brain_Scripts
REF / IDeLife, 2018 · doi:10.7554/eLife.33321
<PKND_003.14.04>
PEER REVIEWED2017fMRIGAMBLING DISORDERCUE REACTIVITY

Neural substrates of cue reactivity and craving in gambling disorder

Limbrick-Oldfield, Mick, Cocks … Lingford-Hughes & Clark · Translational Psychiatry · 2017

Participants with gambling disorder in treatment (n = 19) and 19 controls viewed personally tailored gambling cues, neutral cues and appetitive food images during fMRI. Craving rose after gambling cues in the gambling group, who also showed greater insula and anterior cingulate reactivity to gambling cues but no group difference for food cues.

Within the gambling group, craving correlated positively with gambling-cue activity in bilateral insula and ventral striatum, and negatively with ventral striatum–medial prefrontal connectivity.

CAVEATSSmall groups (19 vs 19), treatment-seeking sample; personally tailored cues improve validity but complicate standardisation.
WHY IT
MATTERS
FOR YOU		A key reference for your gambling cue-reactivity work, with Imperial's Anne Lingford-Hughes among the authors: the insula/ACC and striatal–prefrontal findings give you prior regions and a craving-link analysis to mirror in EEG source space.
SINCE THENStarcke et al. (J Behav Addict, 2018) meta-analysed 18 cue-reactivity studies in gambling, gaming and buying disorders, finding higher subjective (d = 0.84) and physiological/EEG (d = 0.61) cue reactivity in patients. doi:10.1556/2006.7.2018.39
READING LEVELRead the methods
REF / IDTransl Psychiatry, 2017 · doi:10.1038/tp.2016.256
<PKND_003.14.05>
PEER REVIEWED2020METHODSSPATIAL AUTOCORRELATION

Generative modelling of brain maps with spatial autocorrelation (BrainSMASH)

Burt, Helmer, Shinn, Anticevic & Murray (Yale) · NeuroImage · 2020

Spatial autocorrelation (SA) violates the independence assumption behind standard tests when you correlate two brain maps. The authors introduced a generative null model, released as open software, that produces surrogate maps with SA matched to a target map, for cortical, subcortical, parcellated and dense data, and showed how SA inflates p-values in map-to-map comparisons.

Why it mattered: alongside spin tests, it became one of the standard ways to test map correspondences honestly, including in gene-enrichment analyses.

CAVEATSResults still depend on the variogram fit and parcellation; different null models can disagree.
WHY IT
MATTERS
FOR YOU		Directly on your spatial autocorrelation in psychedelic fMRI project: any comparison of a drug-effect map with receptor or gradient maps needs SA-preserving nulls like these.
SINCE THENMarkello & Mišić (NeuroImage, 2021) benchmarked spatial null models, and neuromaps (Nature Methods, 2022) packaged them with a library of brain maps. doi:10.1016/j.neuroimage.2021.118052 · doi:10.1038/s41592-022-01625-w
READING LEVELRead the methods
CODE/DATAbrainsmash (Python) · neuromaps
REF / IDNeuroImage, 2020 · doi:10.1016/j.neuroimage.2020.117038
