<PKND_001.01.01>
PEER REVIEWEDPHASE 2B RCTTRD

One intranasal dose of mebufotenin (5-MeO-DMT) outperforms a low-dose comparator in treatment-resistant depression

Feifel (Kadima Neuropsychiatry Institute), Seynaeve, … Liknaitzky (Monash), … Conley (AtaiBeckley) · JAMA · 26 Oct 2026

A double-blind phase 2b trial at 38 sites in Australia, Europe and the US (Sept 2023–Apr 2025): 193 adults with moderate-to-severe depression unresponsive to 2–5 antidepressants (mean age 41; 59.1% female) received one intranasal dose with psychological support: 8 mg (n = 46), 12 mg (n = 73) or a 0.3 mg comparator (n = 74).

On the primary endpoint, MADRS change at day 29, 12 mg beat 0.3 mg (−11.1 vs −5.5; difference −5.3, 95% CI −8.9 to −1.8; P = .003). The 8 mg dose did at least as well (−12.5; difference −6.2, 95% CI −10.2 to −2.1; P = .003). Response rates at day 29 were 35% (8 mg), 34% (12 mg) and 12% (0.3 mg), and remission rates were 26%, 18% and 8%.

Treatment-emergent adverse events occurred in 76%, 85% and 73% (99.7% mild or moderate). Within 2 hours, 85% (8 mg) and 78% (12 mg) were judged ready for discharge.

CAVEATSIndustry-sponsored (several authors are affiliated with AtaiBeckley or Beckley Psytech; check the COI statement). Blinding a fast, intense psychedelic is hard even with an active low dose. The higher dose didn't beat the lower one, and follow-up is weeks, not long-term.
WHY IT
MATTERS
FOR YOU		A short-acting compound makes acute EEG tractable: record the whole acute arc and return to baseline in one session, and see whether alpha suppression and increased LZ follow a compressed time course.
REF / IDJAMA, 26 Oct 2026 · doi:10.1001/jama.2026.18380 · NCT05870540
<PKND_001.01.02>
PEER REVIEWEDMECHANISMPRECLINICAL

β-arrestin signalling at 5-HT2A may carry sustained antidepressant-like effects without psychedelic-like behaviour (mice and macaques)

He, Tang, Cao, … Cheng & Wang · Nature Neuroscience · 23 Oct 2026

Using structure-guided 5-HT2AR–β-arrestin-biased agonists, the authors argue that β-arrestin signalling, not Gαq, drives prolonged antidepressant-like effects in mice. The key is kinetics: LSD dissociates slowly, so repeated low doses can accumulate receptor engagement close to a single high dose without the peak, sustaining β-arrestin signalling while limiting Gαq-mediated Ca²⁺ responses.

In mice, repeated low-dose LSD, and repeated low-dose psilocybin pretreatment followed by a high-dose challenge, produced sustained antidepressant-like effects. They detected no psychedelic-associated behavioural disruption in mice or cynomolgus macaques.

CAVEATSAnimal behaviour stands in for mood and subjective effects; no behavioural disruption doesn't mean no altered experience. Funded by Chinese state and Shanghai sources (MOST, NSFC, CAS, ShanghaiTech). Several authors declare university patent filings on the new 5-HT2AR agonists, so the biased-agonist claims come from an interested party.
WHY IT
MATTERS
FOR YOU		The JAMA trial shortens the experience; this would remove it. If a dosing schedule avoided acute Gαq-type effects, you'd predict little acute alpha or complexity change but some post-acute change, which your acute vs post-acute EEG designs could test.
REF / IDNat Neurosci, 23 Oct 2026 · doi:10.1038/s41593-026-02497-5
 
 ICYMI, pairs with He et al.: plasticity without the head twitch (Nat Neurosci, 15 Oct). Baker, Kogan, Ma, Lu & Zuo. 5-HT2A signalling in layer 5 pyramidal neurons was necessary and sufficient for psilocybin's synaptic remodelling but not for the head-twitch response: a cell-type route to the separation He et al. pursue via signalling. doi:10.1038/s41593-026-02490-y

<PKND_001.02.01>
PREPRINTEEGPSILOCYBINCONTEXT

The setting changes the EEG signature: context reshapes psilocybin's alpha-band connectivity effects

Dhanawansa, Stoliker, Novelli, Biabani, Razi & Kuhlmann (Monash) · bioRxiv · posted 16 Oct 2026

Described by the authors as the largest psilocybin EEG dataset to date (n = 56), with matched pre-drug baselines, recorded during movie viewing, eyes-closed rest, guided meditation and music. Source-space power, alpha peak frequency and coherence were analysed within and between seven networks across six bands (1–80 Hz), with FDR-corrected permutation tests and drug × context mixed models.

At rest, psilocybin decreased alpha power, increased gamma-range power and raised alpha peak frequency, and alpha-band connectivity fell within and between higher-order control networks. Structured sensory contexts attenuated or even reversed these alpha-connectivity changes, depending on the context.

CAVEATSPreprint; effect sizes aren't in the abstract. Source-space coherence is still vulnerable to leakage (see Dimou, below); check dose and design in the full text.
WHY IT
MATTERS
FOR YOU		Squarely your territory. If context can reverse an alpha-connectivity effect, cross-study comparisons need context matched or modelled, and 'eyes-closed rest' isn't a neutral default.

<PKND_001.03.01>
PEER REVIEWEDREANALYSISDEPRESSION

Psilocybin vs escitalopram by sex: a Bayesian reanalysis of the Imperial trial

Frick, Blest-Hopley, Girn, Erritzoe, Nutt & Carhart-Harris · Psychopharmacology · 26 Oct 2026

A post hoc Bayesian secondary analysis of the six-week double-blind psilocybin vs escitalopram RCT (N = 59, 20 female; psilocybin n = 30 with 11 female, escitalopram n = 29 with 9 female). There was no overall sex difference on any outcome, and none on the depression scales.

Within the psilocybin arm, women had lower post-treatment anxiety than men (STAI adjusted difference −8.77, 95% CrI −15.73 to −1.73, BF 11.18), but this was prior-sensitive and the narrowest prior included zero. The sex × treatment interaction was inconclusive (−8.62, 95% CrI −18.36 to 1.30, BF 2.55). Sexual dysfunction was lower with psilocybin among men, and lower in women than men on escitalopram.

CAVEATSA secondary analysis of a trial that wasn't powered for sex effects, with 9–11 women per arm. Results are prior-sensitive, so treat them as hypothesis-generating.
WHY IT
MATTERS
FOR YOU		Reviewers increasingly expect sex to be modelled, not just reported. Pre-specifying how sex (and, where feasible, hormonal status) enters your EEG models costs little now.
REF / IDPsychopharmacology, 26 Oct 2026 · doi:10.1007/s00213-026-07177-7

<PKND_001.04.01>
PREPRINTfMRIPSILOCYBINSELF

Language networks and the loss of self: inter-subject similarity in language regions tracks ego dissolution under psilocybin

Cooper, Stoliker, Astl Delgado, Blackburne, Novelli, Green, Razi, Das & Skipper (UCL) · bioRxiv · posted 14 Oct 2026

Participants heard music and watched moving clouds in the scanner at baseline and after 19 mg psilocybin. The authors tested whether inter-subject similarity of brain activity increased with each pair's summed Ego Dissolution Inventory (EDI) score (N = 43), using elementary visual imagery (N = 49) and baseline as comparators.

Under psilocybin, one component group formed a left-lateralised frontotemporal network matching the Neurosynth language map (r = .382, spatial-null p < .001, q = .003), nearly double the strongest comparator (imagery r = .208). People with higher EDI showed larger changes (rho = .455, Bonferroni p = .048), most strongly for items about loss of ego or identity. Exploratory resting-state analyses showed this network decoupling from posterior visual cortex and coupling more with thalamic and frontal regions.

CAVEATSPreprint. The brain–EDI correlation only just survives correction, and decoding with Neurosynth maps is a reverse inference.
WHY IT
MATTERS
FOR YOU		A testable mechanism for ego dissolution that EEG could address, e.g. by tracking language-network activity or speech in acute sessions. Blackburne is an author, linking it to the Phi-ID work.
REF / IDbioRxiv, 14 Oct 2026 · doi:10.64898/2026.10.06.757069
<PKND_001.04.02>
PREPRINTfMRILSD + DMTMODELLING

LSD and DMT slow the haemodynamic response, and correcting for it sharpens connectivity effects

Mundel, Medel, Timmermann, … Deco, Ibanez, Carhart-Harris, Sanz Perl & Tagliazucchi · bioRxiv · posted 16 Oct 2026

Blind deconvolution of resting BOLD showed that both LSD and DMT increased estimated haemodynamic delays, in regions overlapping serotonin receptors involved in both psychedelic action and vascular regulation.

Deconvolution improved detection of drug-induced connectivity changes, suggesting vascular effects obscure real changes rather than create spurious ones. A whole-brain model linked the delay to stronger flow-dependent feedback and slower dissipation of vasoactive drive.

CAVEATSPreprint. Haemodynamic delay is estimated, not directly measured, and sample sizes aren't in the abstract.
WHY IT
MATTERS
FOR YOU		Psychedelic fMRI analyses should probably account for drug-altered neurovascular coupling, and it's a strong argument for EEG as the reference measure in multimodal designs.

05  Complexity and dynamics
SEC.05  /  complexity, criticality and methods worth knowing
<PKND_001.05.01>
PREPRINTMODELLINGCOMPLEXITY

Which complexity measures actually track network structure? Perturbational ones, in a modelling study from Massimini's group

Gaglioti, Cattani, Nieus, Momi, Comolatti, Dalla Porta, Sarasso & Massimini (University of Milan) · bioRxiv · posted 22 Oct 2026

They simulated Wilson–Cowan mean-field networks, rewiring connectivity step by step to grade the structural balance of integration and differentiation, and asked which complexity metrics track it.

Observational metrics (from spontaneous activity) behaved inconsistently: some tracked integration, some differentiation, some barely responded. Perturbational metrics (from the response to a simulated pulse) peaked at intermediate rewiring, scaled with small-worldness, and were highest in a wake-like regime near critical instability.

From the full text: across rewiring from ring to random to modular networks (10 realisations per step), spontaneous LZc and ACE didn't change significantly. Under a common driving input, observational metrics deviated far more than perturbational ones (mean absolute deviation: LZc 22.4%, ACE 9.3%, SCE 15.8%, neural complexity 43.0%, Fcomp 16.0%, vs PCI 3.8%, dimensionality 4.4%, ⟨H⟩ 1.6%, H_rate 2.5%).

CAVEATSA simulation study, so the conclusions hold only as far as the model class generalises. Preprint (v2 posted 26 Oct; numbers from the v1 full text).
WHY IT
MATTERS
FOR YOU		Much psychedelic EEG work rests on spontaneous LZ. If observational measures shift for reasons unrelated to network structure, such as common inputs, 'more complex' needs care; pair them with a perturbational readout (e.g. TMS-EEG) where you can.
REF / IDbioRxiv, 22 Oct 2026 · doi:10.64898/2026.10.21.760743
<PKND_001.05.02>
PREPRINTLFP + fMRIANAESTHESIATRAVELLING WAVES

Going under and coming back follow different paths: scale-dependent reversibility in propofol anaesthesia

Zhang, Han, Chen, … Cramer & Ding (Penn State) · Research Square · posted 27 Oct 2026

Rats under graded propofol were recorded with simultaneous local electrophysiology, whole-brain fMRI and pupillometry. LFPs, regional BOLD and pairwise FC changed largely reversibly between loss and recovery of consciousness.

Global organisation didn't: travelling waves, low-dimensional network trajectories and graph measures took asymmetric paths down and back. Pupil–BOLD coupling stayed consistent, so the authors argue neuromodulatory tone doesn't explain the asymmetry and that recovery involves active large-scale reorganisation, not just the drug wearing off.

CAVEATSPreprint, rodents, one anaesthetic; sample and effect sizes aren't in the abstract.
WHY IT
MATTERS
FOR YOU		Travelling-wave and trajectory analysis of a consciousness-altering drug. The hysteresis question carries straight over: does the EEG trajectory at psychedelic onset retrace itself at offset? Feature-distance approaches like Junheng Li's suit that test.

06  Weekly explainer
SEC.06  /  observational vs perturbational complexity, and where Phi-ID fits
EXPLAINERINFO THEORY 101
 

Lempel–Ziv complexity (LZc) is what most psychedelic EEG papers report. You binarise each signal (e.g. above or below its median) and count new patterns as you scan along it, essentially asking how compressible it is. More new patterns means higher LZc. It measures the diversity of a signal over time, and it's observational: computed from spontaneous activity.

The catch is that many things make a signal less compressible. Broadband noise, a weaker alpha rhythm or a change in a region's input can all raise LZc without any change in wiring, which is the point of the Massimini paper above.

Perturbational complexity (PCI) stimulates the cortex (classically with TMS), records how the evoked response spreads across channels and time, and compresses that pattern. A high score needs the response to spread widely (integration) and stay differentiated rather than one uniform wave. Because it measures the response to a push, it tends to reflect architecture more faithfully.

Phi-ID, used by Blackburne et al. and the global-workspace preprint above, splits the information two regions carry about their future into redundancy (available from either alone) and synergy (only available from both together), a more principled cousin of 'integration'. So LZc asks how diverse the signal is, PCI how the system responds to a perturbation, and Phi-ID how information is shared or combined. They're complementary, not interchangeable.

<PKND_001.07.01>
PEER REVIEWEDRCT SUBSTUDYfMRINICOTINE

Adding the GLP-1 agonist dulaglutide to varenicline didn't change outcomes in smokers, clinically or in the brain

Sailer, Lengsfeld, Coynel, … Christ-Crain & Winzeler (University Hospital Basel) · Brain Communications · 27 Oct 2026

A predefined imaging substudy of an RCT (parent n = 255): 71 smokers were randomised to 1.5 mg dulaglutide (n = 35) or placebo (n = 36) for 12 weeks on top of varenicline and counselling, with structural and functional MRI at baseline and week 12 (59 completed).

Abstinence was 54% with dulaglutide vs 68% with placebo (p = 0.27). Craving fell over time in everyone (p < 0.001) and working memory improved in quitters (p = 0.02), with no group differences on any clinical, functional or structural outcome.

CAVEATSAn add-on design against strong standard care, so a ceiling effect is plausible. Modest sample size for a between-group imaging comparison.
WHY IT
MATTERS
FOR YOU		Where nicotine addiction meets metabolic psychiatry, and a benchmark for your psychedelic nicotine study: varenicline plus counselling is a demanding comparator, and craving fell in both arms, so controls must absorb time and expectancy effects.
REF / IDBrain Commun, 27 Oct 2026 · doi:10.1093/braincomms/fcag410

Pais, Tikochinski, King, … Goldstein & Reichart (Technion and others) · medRxiv · posted 26 Oct 2026

Food addiction: theta-band stimulation of a dACC-centred network changes how the FRN encodes reward prediction errors

Wen, Wang, Dong, Gola, Liu, Tu & Li (Institute of Psychology, CAS, and others) · Neuroscience Bulletin · 20 Oct 2026

A single-blind, sham-controlled pre–post study in people with elevated food-addiction symptoms, who did a food incentive-delay task with EEG during theta-band TI stimulation targeting a dACC-centred network.

Single-trial FRN amplitude varied with behavioural and model-derived prediction errors; theta stimulation altered FRN–prediction-error coupling, most for unfavourable outcomes, while anticipatory activity was unchanged.

MATTERS

Psilocin alters interval timing in mice via 5-HT2A, and medial prefrontal cortex is sufficient to reproduce the effect

Klein, Kopelman, Sharp, Powell, Geyer, Young & Halberstadt (UC San Diego / VA San Diego) · Psychopharmacology · 19 Oct 2026

Male C57BL/6J mice judged whether a light lasted shorter or longer than 6.5 s. Psilocin (0.8 mg/kg SC) altered timing accuracy and precision; the 5-HT2A antagonist M100907 blocked this, the 5-HT1A antagonist WAY-100,635 didn't.

DOI microinfused into mPFC mimicked systemic DOI, and 5-HT2A inverse agonists also shifted timing, suggesting both more and less 5-HT2A activation can change the perceived 'clock speed'.

CAVEATSMale mice only, one psilocin dose, and a task far from human subjective time. No competing interests declared (NIDA funded).
WHY IT
MATTERS
FOR YOU		Altered time perception is a core acute effect you can probe with EEG (timing tasks, frontal oscillations, CNV-like potentials). This gives a receptor- and region-level hypothesis to test in humans.

PREPRINTPET-MRI-EEGNULL

No detectable change in synaptic density or plasticity markers 1–8 days after DMT or ketamine (Imperial multimodal study)

Agnorelli, Peill, Kurtin, … Carhart-Harris, Matthews, Rabiner, Nutt & Erritzoe (Imperial and others) · bioRxiv · posted 13 Oct 2026 (ICYMI)

18 healthy volunteers underwent [11C]-UCB-J PET (SV2A synaptic density), diffusion MRI with NODDI, resting-state fMRI and EEG plasticity tasks (roving mismatch negativity and visual LTP), before and after ketamine (n = 11) or DMT (n = 7).

There were no statistically significant changes 1–8 days after either drug in SV2A binding, neurite orientation dispersion, structural weighted degree, MMN repetition suppression or late-phase vLTP P2 potentiation. At baseline, SV2A binding was coupled with neurite orientation dispersion, frontotemporal SV2A binding correlated with MMN repetition suppression (r = 0.61; 0.49 after the drug), and occipital binding correlated with visual LTP P2 potentiation (r = 0.54).

CAVEATSVery small groups (11 and 7), so 'no significant change' isn't 'no change'. Healthy volunteers, one 1–8 day window. Preprint.
WHY IT
MATTERS
FOR YOU		A reality check on human plasticity claims. The EEG paradigms (MMN and visual LTP) overlap with your task-EEG interests, and the variability could inform power calculations.
