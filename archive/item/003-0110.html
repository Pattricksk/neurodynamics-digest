Five labs, one protocol: psilocybin's acute effects in mice replicate, most 24-hour 'antidepressant-like' effects don't

Lu OD, Malenka RC
Nature Neuroscience · 2026-11-02 · doi:10.1038/s41593-026-02500-z
PsilocybinAnxietyPre-clinicalPeer reviewedmulti-lab replicationbehavioural assaysmice#replication#persistent effects#anxiety#fear extinction

Five independent laboratories ran the same experiments on psilocybin (2 mg/kg i.p.), using about 200 male and female mice per experiment. Psilocybin robustly and replicably increased anxiety- and avoidance-related behaviour and decreased fear expression acutely. But at 24 hours it did not replicably reduce anxiety- or depression-like behaviour or facilitate fear extinction, and it didn't alter social preference or social reward learning. The authors conclude that some reported persistent effects in mice may be modest and/or inconsistent.

CAVEATS One dose and route, and a specific behavioural battery; null replication of behaviour doesn't speak directly to structural plasticity or to human efficacy.
