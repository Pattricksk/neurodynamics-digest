Generative modelling of brain maps with spatial autocorrelation (BrainSMASH)

Burt JB, Murray JD
NeuroImage · 2020-10 · doi:10.1016/j.neuroimage.2020.117038
fMRIPeer reviewedspatial null modelssurrogate mapsvariogram#spatial autocorrelation#brain maps#statistics#catch-up

Spatial autocorrelation (SA) violates the independence assumption behind standard tests when you correlate two brain maps. The authors introduced a generative null model, released as open software, that produces surrogate maps with SA matched to a target map, for cortical, subcortical, parcellated and dense data, and showed how SA inflates p-values in map-to-map comparisons. Why it mattered: alongside spin tests, it became one of the standard ways to test map correspondences honestly, including in gene-enrichment analyses.

CAVEATS Results still depend on the variogram fit and parcellation; different null models can disagree.
