Neural substrates of cue reactivity and craving in gambling disorder

Limbrick-Oldfield EH, Clark L
Translational Psychiatry · 2017-01-03 · doi:10.1038/tp.2016.256
fMRIGamblingHealthy volunteersClinicalPeer reviewedtask fMRIcue reactivityfunctional connectivityadults with gambling disorderhealthy controls#gambling#cue reactivity#craving#addiction neuroimaging#catch-up

Participants with gambling disorder in treatment (n = 19) and 19 controls viewed personally tailored gambling cues, neutral cues and appetitive food images during fMRI. Craving rose after gambling cues in the gambling group, who also showed greater insula and anterior cingulate reactivity to gambling cues but no group difference for food cues. Within the gambling group, craving correlated positively with gambling-cue activity in bilateral insula and ventral striatum, and negatively with ventral striatum–medial prefrontal connectivity.

CAVEATS Small groups (19 vs 19), treatment-seeking sample; personally tailored cues improve validity but complicate standardisation.
