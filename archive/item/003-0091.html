Psilocybin's rapid antidepressant effect came without a group-level rise in SV2A, but baseline SV2A predicted who improved

Yngwe H, Lundberg J
medRxiv (preprint) · 2026-11-08 · doi:10.64898/2026.11.06.26367247
PsilocybinPETDepression (incl. TRD/MDD)ClinicalPreprintSV2A PET[11C]UCB-J PETRCTadults with major depressive disorder#plasticity#synaptic density#treatment prediction#depression

A pre-registered [11C]UCB-J PET sub-study of a published randomised trial. Thirty-five people with moderate-to-severe recurrent major depression were randomised 1:1 to a single 25 mg psilocybin dose or 100 mg niacin as active placebo, both with psychotherapeutic support. PET ran 2–7 days before and 15 (±7) days after dosing, quantifying SV2A (a synaptic vesicle protein used as a proxy for synaptic density) in hippocampus and frontal cortex. Despite significant clinical improvement in the psilocybin group, there was no evidence of a group-level SV2A increase relative to niacin (VT or BPND). CAVEATS Preprint. Small PET sample (n = 35 across two arms). SV2A is an indirect marker of synaptic density, and PET resolution can't see local or cell-type-specific remodelling; the scan was about two weeks after dosing, so a transient change earlier on can't be ruled out. The baseline-predicts-response finding is secondary and needs replication.
