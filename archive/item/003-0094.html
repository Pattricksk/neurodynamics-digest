How often can you actually see an MMN in one person? A benchmark in healthy controls and minimally conscious patients

Pavlov YG, Kotchoubey B
medRxiv (preprint) · 2026-11-02 · doi:10.64898/2026.10.31.26366991
EEGDisorders of consciousnessHealthy volunteersClinicalPreprintEEG ERPsMMNP300N400SVM decodinghealthy controlspatients in minimally conscious state#MMN#disorders of consciousness#single-subject reliability

48 healthy controls and 26 patients in a minimally conscious state (MCS) completed six auditory paradigms: multifeature MMN, passive and active oddball P300, own-name, semantic oddball and sentence N400. Effects were classified with blinded expert visual scoring, ROI ANOVA, cluster-based permutation and SVM decoding. In controls, visual scoring found an MMN to at least one deviant and a P300 in both oddball tasks in all 48; task-related P300 enhancement (command following) was detected in 81%, sentence-congruity effects in 69% and the own-name effect in only 35%. Patients showed lower detection: MMN in 27% for the short-duration deviant and 65% when any of five deviant types counted. Prognos

CAVEATS Preprint; modest patient sample, and detection rates depend on which classification method you choose.
