Single Intranasal Administration of the Psychedelic Mebufotenin for Treatment-Resistant Depression

David Feifel, Mathieu Seynaeve, Wiesław Jerzy Cubała, Fiona Dunbar, Gerhard Gründer, Rosa María Dueñas Herrero, Chandni Hindocha, Paul Liknaitzky, Sean M. McGuigan, Sejal Mehta, Mimi Pierce, Dimitris Repantis, Claire Roberts, Frank Wiegand, Robert Conley
JAMA · 2026-10-26 · doi:10.1001/jama.2026.18380
5-MeO-DMT (mebufotenin)Depression (incl. TRD/MDD)Clinicalphase 2b RCTadults with treatment-resistant depression#TRD#short-acting psychedelic#clinical trial

A double-blind phase 2b trial at 38 sites in Australia, Europe and the US (Sept 2023–Apr 2025): 193 adults with moderate-to-severe depression unresponsive to 2–5 antidepressants (mean age 41; 59.1% female) received one intranasal dose with psychological support: 8 mg (n = 46), 12 mg (n = 73) or a 0.3 mg comparator (n = 74).

CAVEATS Industry-sponsored (several authors are affiliated with AtaiBeckley or Beckley Psytech; check the COI statement). Blinding a fast, intense psychedelic is hard even with an active low dose. The higher dose didn't beat the lower one, and follow-up is weeks, not long-term.
