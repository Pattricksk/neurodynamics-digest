LSD in simultaneous PET-MRI: high 5-HT2A occupancy, more cerebral blood flow, less global connectivity

McCulloch DE, Knudsen GM
Nature Neuroscience · 2026-10-12 · doi:10.1038/s41593-026-02462-2
LSDPsilocybinfMRIPETHealthy volunteersClinicalPeer reviewedPET-MRIfMRICBF (ASL)5-HT2A occupancyhealthy volunteers#receptor occupancy#haemodynamics#functional connectivity

Healthy volunteers, blinded to dose, had simultaneous PET-MRI after LSD. LSD occupied 5-HT2A receptors with high affinity, increased global cerebral blood flow and internal carotid flow, and decreased global functional connectivity; the connectivity decrease was negatively associated with the CBF change. Subjective effects showed anticlockwise hysteresis against plasma levels. A separate psilocybin cohort (n = 25) showed the opposite pattern: lower CBF and higher global FC.

CAVEATS Small sample for the LSD PET arm. The opposite LSD/psilocybin haemodynamic patterns complicate comparing BOLD effects across drugs.
