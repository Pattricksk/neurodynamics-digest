Stomach–brain synchrony reveals a delayed-connectivity resting-state network

Rebollo I, Tallon-Baudry C
eLife · 2018-03-21 · doi:10.7554/elife.33321
fMRIHealthy volunteersClinicalPeer reviewedresting-state fMRIEGGphase-locking valuehealthy adults#brain-body coupling#gastric network#catch-up

Using simultaneous resting fMRI and electrogastrography, the authors found a 'gastric network': brain regions phase-synchronised with the stomach's slow (~0.05 Hz) electrical rhythm. It cuts across classical resting-state networks, partly overlaps autonomic regions, and is dominated by areas that map bodily and external space (somatomotor cortices through to the extrastriate body area and dorsal precuneus), with a precise temporal sequence within each gastric cycle. Why it mattered: it showed that resting-state organisation isn't only about instantaneous connectivity, and put the stomach on the map as a driver of slow brain dynamics.

CAVEATS Healthy participants only; EGG is noisy, and the phase-locking approach needs careful surrogate statistics.
