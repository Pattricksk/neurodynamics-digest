The path from brain to words is rerouted in opioid addiction

Shani Pais, Refael Tikochinski, Sarah G King, Nitay Calderon, Rita Z Goldstein, Roi Reichart
medRxiv (preprint) · 2026-10-26 · doi:10.64898/2026.10.24.26366318
fMRIOpioid use disorderClinicalnaturalistic fMRIlanguage modelspeople with opioid use disordercontrols#opioids#drug cues#addiction neuroimaging

82 people with opioid use disorder (OUD) and 34 controls watched the opening of Trainspotting in fMRI, then retold it; people with OUD showed a speech bias toward the drug scenes.

CAVEATS Preprint; imaging subsample of 65, cross-sectional, small control group, no test statistics in the abstract.
