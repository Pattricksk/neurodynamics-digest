Psilocybin’s lasting action requires pyramidal cell types and 5-HT2A receptors

Ling-Xiao Shao, Clara Liao, Pasha A. Davoudian, Neil K. Savalia, Quan Jiang, Cassandra Wojtasiewicz, Diran Tan, Jack D. Nothnagel, Rong-Jian Liu, Samuel C. Woodburn, Olesia M. Bilash, Hail Kim, Alicia Che, Alex C. Kwan
Nature · 2025-04-02 · doi:10.1038/s41586-025-08813-6
PsilocybinPre-clinicaltwo-photon imagingchemogeneticscell-type-specific electrophysiologyconditional 5-HT2A knockoutmice#structural plasticity#dendritic spines#medial frontal cortex#pyramidal tract neurons#5-HT2A receptor#stress#mechanism of action

In mouse medial frontal cortex, one dose of psilocybin grew dendritic spines on both pyramidal-tract (subcortical-projecting) and intratelencephalic neurons, but only the pyramidal-tract cells mattered for behaviour: silencing them abolished psilocybin's anti-stress effects, and only they showed boosted synaptic calcium signals and firing acutely. Knocking out 5-HT2A receptors in this region wiped out both the plasticity and the behavioural benefit.

CAVEATS Mouse-only, with stress-related behaviours as a stand-in for depression; one brain region and cell-type silencing rather than human data. Strong mechanistic evidence that 5-HT2A matters for the lasting effects in mice, but doesn't settle whether the trip itself is needed in humans.
