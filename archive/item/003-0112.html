doi:10.1021/acschemneuro.6c00743 (Does Gi signalling separate hallucinogenic from non-hallucinogenic 5-HT2A agonists? Two papers disagree.)

Hatzipantelis CJ, Olson DE
ACS Chemical Neuroscience · 2026-10-17 · doi:10.1021/acschemneuro.6c00743
Non-hal (5-HT2A agonists)Pre-clinicalPeer reviewedreanalysis of source data#5-HT2A signalling#Gi#non-hallucinogenic#critique

Miranda-Azpiazu, … González-Maeso & Meana (Neurochem Int, 6 Nov), in post-mortem human prefrontal cortex: DOI, TCB-2 and LSD activated Gq/11 and Gi1 via 5-HT2A, while non-hallucinogenic Ariadne, 2-Br-LSD and lisuride activated Gq/11 but not Gi1; they note a larger drug panel is needed. In the other corner (ICYMI, ACS Chem Neurosci, 17 Oct), Hatzipantelis & Olson reanalysed source data from an earlier Gi paper by Xu et al. and argue Gi activation is less predictive of hallucinogenic potential than canonical 5-HT2A effectors. Unresolved, and relevant to how non-hallucinogenic 'psychoplastogens' are defined.
